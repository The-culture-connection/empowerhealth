Iron and Anemia in Pregnancy

What This Is

Iron is a mineral your body uses to make hemoglobin, the part of your red blood cells that carries oxygen. During pregnancy your blood volume grows, so you need more iron than usual.

When your iron runs low, you can develop iron-deficiency anemia. It is common in the second trimester, and it can be treated.

Why It Matters for Your Health

  • Enough iron helps you feel less tired and less short of breath.
  • It supports your baby's growth and the placenta.
  • It helps your body get ready for birth and recovery.

What to Expect

Your care team checks your blood count with a simple blood test. If your level is low, they may suggest an iron supplement and a repeat test a few weeks later.

  • Iron pills can cause constipation, an upset stomach, or dark stools. Tell your provider if they are hard to take.
  • Foods with vitamin C, like oranges or orange juice, can help your body absorb iron.

What You Can Ask or Say

  • “What was my hemoglobin level, and what should it be?”
  • “How and when should I take my iron?”
  • “What can I do if the pills upset my stomach?”

When to Seek Medical Help

Call your provider if you feel very weak or dizzy, your heart is racing, or you are short of breath while resting. If you have chest pain, faint, or have trouble breathing, call 911.

Key Points

  • Low iron is common in pregnancy and can be treated.
  • Take your supplement the way your provider recommends.
  • Your repeat blood work on November 4 will show how it is working.
